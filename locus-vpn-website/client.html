The app

One window, and almost nothing in it

The client exists to answer one question — am I connected, and on which tier — and then get out of the way. There is no dashboard to learn and no upsell inside the app.

Behaviour

What the client does on its own

Reconnects without asking

If the tunnel drops, the client re-establishes it and picks the fastest reachable server. You see the state change; you do not have to click anything.

Shows its work

Measured round-trip time, the server in use and the active speed cap are on the main screen. If something feels slow, the client tells you which of those is responsible.

Stays quiet about your traffic

The client reports connection state and nothing else — no destinations, no traffic contents, no per-app usage. Diagnostics are generated locally and only sent if you send them.

Collects diagnostics you can read

One button produces a plain-text report: version, platform, activation state, tier, engine status and recent heartbeat results. Copy it into a support email rather than describing symptoms.

Trade-offs

Choices you might disagree with

Three decisions in the client are deliberate, and each has a downside worth stating.

Closing the window quits the app

There is no system tray icon, so a closed window cannot leave the tunnel running invisibly. The cost is that minimising to the tray is not possible.

No in-app plan upsell

If you are on Basic, the client will not suggest Gaming. Upgrade prompts live on the website, not in the place you opened to check your connection.

Managed servers only

You cannot paste a third-party server or a custom config. That keeps the network consistent and support tractable, but it does mean no self-hosted endpoints.
